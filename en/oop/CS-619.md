# Stock order book with matching

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Classes and OOP](../oop/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/), [Errors and exceptions](../errors/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-619?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Implement an order book for one stock, empty at first. You get a list of operations `operations` and a list of their arguments `values` (`values[i]` belongs to `operations[i]`). Return a list of results, one per operation.

Operations:
- `"buy"` with arguments `id`, `price`, `qty`: an order to buy `qty` shares at a price of at most `price`. It is matched at once against sell orders priced at most `price`: the lowest price first, and at an equal price the earlier order first. Each trade happens at the resting order's price for the smaller of the two remaining quantities. What is left unfilled rests in the book. Result: how many shares this operation bought.
- `"sell"` with the same arguments: symmetric, against buy orders priced at least `price`, the highest price first, and at an equal price the earlier one first. Result: how many shares were sold.
- `"cancel"` with argument `id`: remove what is left of the order from the book. Result: the removed quantity.
- `"best_bid"`: the highest buy price in the book, `0` if there are no buy orders.
- `"best_ask"`: the lowest sell price in the book, `0` if none.
- `"depth"` with argument `price`: the total quantity resting in the book at that price (`0` if none).

Among orders at one price, the one whose remainder entered the book earlier comes first. If `id` was already used in this call, or `price` ≤ 0, or `qty` ≤ 0, or `cancel` names an order that is not in the book (unknown, fully filled or already cancelled), report an error: throw an exception, or return an error in Go. The error applies to the whole call: the first bad operation aborts it. Up to 1000 operations.

Example: `["sell", "sell", "buy", "best_ask"]`, `[[1, 10, 5], [2, 10, 3], [3, 10, 6], []]` → `[0, 0, 6, 10]`.
