# Team calendar and a common free slot

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Classes and OOP](../oop/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-629?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Implement a team calendar where everyone is free at first. A person is a number and a time is an integer; the interval `[s, e)` (`s` < `e`) covers the moments `s` to `e − 1`. You get a list of operations `operations` and a list of their arguments `values` (`values[i]` belongs to `operations[i]`). Return a list of results, one per operation.

Operations:
- `"busy"` with arguments `person`, `s`, `e`: the person is busy during `[s, e)` (overlapping already busy time is allowed). Result: how many moments the person has busy in total.
- `"free"` with arguments `person`, `s`, `e`: free the interval `[s, e)` for the person. Result: how many moments the person has busy in total.
- `"slot"` with arguments `length`, `from`, then the people's numbers (at least one, no repeats): the earliest moment `t` ≥ `from` such that all the listed people are free during `[t, t + length)`. Time has no upper bound, so there is always an answer.
- `"book"` with the same arguments: find `t` as `slot` does and make `[t, t + length)` busy for all the listed people. Result: `t`.
- `"busy_at"` with arguments `person`, `t`: `1` if the person is busy at moment `t`, otherwise `0`.

All moments and lengths in the arguments are 0 to 10⁵ (`length` ≥ 1), with up to 2000 operations.

Example: `["busy", "busy", "busy", "slot"]`, `[[1, 9, 12], [2, 10, 11], [2, 13, 15], [2, 9, 1, 2]]` → `[3, 1, 3, 15]`.
