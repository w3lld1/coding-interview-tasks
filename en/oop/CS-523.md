# Mail tray from two piles

**Difficulty:** easy · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Classes and OOP](../oop/), [Stacks, queues, linked lists](../stacks-lists/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-523?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A mail tray works as a queue made of two piles of envelopes: envelopes are put on the inbox pile and taken from the outbox pile. An envelope is an integer. Both piles start empty. Operations:
- `put` (x): put envelope x on top of the inbox pile; the result is the total number of envelopes in both piles;
- `get`: take the oldest envelope of the queue (first in, first out); the result is its number, or `-1` if there are no envelopes;
- `peek`: the same, but the envelope stays; the result is the number of the oldest envelope, or `-1`;
- `moved`: the result is how many envelopes in total have been moved from the inbox pile to the outbox pile so far.

The moving rule: when `get` or `peek` finds the outbox pile empty, all envelopes of the inbox pile are taken one by one from its top and put onto the top of the outbox pile (this reverses their order), and each such move counts in `moved`. If the outbox pile is not empty, nothing is moved.

Return the list of results, one per operation. `values[i]` holds the arguments of operation `i` (an empty list if there are none).

Example: `["put", "put", "put", "get", "moved", "put", "get", "moved", "get", "moved", "get", "moved", "get", "moved"]`, `[[1], [2], [3], [], [], [4], [], [], [], [], [], [], [], []]` → `[1, 2, 3, 1, 3, 3, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, -1, 4]`.
