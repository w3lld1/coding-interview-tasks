# Second-price auction with retracted bids

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Classes and OOP](../oop/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/), [Hash maps and sets](../hashing/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-626?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Implement an auction site, with no bids at first. A lot and a bidder are numbers ≥ 0, and a bid is an integer ≥ 1. Each bidder has at most one live bid on each lot. You get a list of operations `operations` and a list of their arguments `values` (`values[i]` belongs to `operations[i]`). Return a list of results, one per operation.

The leader of a lot is the bidder with the highest live bid; among equal bids, the one whose bid was placed earlier (a bid's moment is the `bid` operation that placed it).

Operations:
- `"bid"` with arguments `item`, `user`, `amount`: the bidder bids `amount` on the lot. Their earlier bid on this lot, if any, is replaced by the new one (even a smaller one), and the bid's moment becomes now. Result: the lot's leader after the operation.
- `"retract"` with arguments `item`, `user`: the bidder withdraws their bid on the lot (nothing happens if there is none). Result: the lot's leader after the operation, or `-1` if no bids are left.
- `"leader"` with argument `item`: the lot's leader, or `-1` if it has no bids.
- `"price"` with argument `item`: what the leader would pay under the second-price rule: the highest live bid of the other bidders; if the leader is the only bidder, their own bid; `-1` if there are no bids.
- `"leads"` with argument `user`: on how many lots this bidder leads now.

Up to 2000 operations.

Example: `["bid", "bid", "bid", "price", "retract", "leader"]`, `[[1, 10, 50], [1, 20, 70], [1, 30, 60], [1], [1, 20], [1]]` → `[10, 20, 20, 60, 30, 30]`.
