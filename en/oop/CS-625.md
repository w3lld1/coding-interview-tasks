# Taxi dispatcher on a straight road

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Classes and OOP](../oop/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-625?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Implement a taxi dispatcher for cars on one straight road. At first there are no drivers. A driver is a number ≥ 0, a position is an integer (absolute value up to 10⁹), and a radius `r` is 0 to 10⁹. A driver is free (waiting at their position), busy (carrying a passenger) or offline. You get a list of operations `operations` and a list of their arguments `values` (`values[i]` belongs to `operations[i]`). Return a list of results, one per operation.

Operations:
- `"online"` with arguments `driver`, `pos`: an offline driver comes online, free at position `pos`. Result: the number of free drivers.
- `"request"` with arguments `pos`, `r`: a passenger at position `pos` will wait for a driver at distance at most `r`. They get the nearest such free driver; at an equal distance the one with the smaller position, then the smaller number. The driver becomes busy. Result: the driver's number, or `-1` if none fits.
- `"drop"` with arguments `driver`, `pos`: a busy driver drops the passenger at position `pos` and becomes free there. Result: the number of free drivers.
- `"offline"` with argument `driver`: the driver (free or busy) goes offline. Result: the number of free drivers.
- `"near"` with arguments `pos`, `r`: how many free drivers wait at distance at most `r` from `pos`.

Operations are always valid: `online` gets an offline driver, `drop` a busy one and `offline` one who is online. Several drivers may wait at one position. Up to 2000 operations.

Example: `["online", "online", "online", "request", "request"]`, `[[1, 0], [2, 10], [3, 4], [6, 5], [6, 5]]` → `[1, 2, 3, 3, 2]`.
