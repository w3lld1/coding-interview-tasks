# Monthly rules over ten thousand years

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Dates and time](../dates/), [Classes and OOP](../oop/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-709?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Implement a store of monthly rules. A rule fires at most once a month:
- `"day D"`: on day `D` of the month (`D` from 1 to 31 without leading zeros; it does not fire in a month without that day);
- `"day D W"`: on day `D`, only if it falls on the weekday `W` (`Mo`, `Tu`, `We`, `Th`, `Fr`, `Sa`, `Su`), for example `"day 13 Fr"`;
- `"nth K W"`: on the `K`-th weekday `W` of the month, `K` from 1 to 5 (no firing if there is no fifth) or `-1` for the last one;
- `"last"`: on the last day of the month.
Words are separated by one space.

You get a list of operations `operations` and a list of their arguments `values` (`values[i]` belongs to `operations[i]`; all arguments are strings). Return a list of results, one per operation. At first there are no rules.

- `"add"` `[id, rule]`: store the rule, result `"ok"`.
- `"skip"` `[id, date]`: if the rule fires on `date` and that date is not skipped yet, skip it (it no longer counts), result `"ok"`, otherwise `"none"`.
- `"count"` `[id, from, to]`: the number of non-skipped firings on the dates from `from` to `to` inclusive, as a decimal string.
- `"next"` `[id, date]`: the first non-skipped firing not before `date`, or `"none"` if there is none up to `9999-12-31`.

The calendar is Gregorian, extended back to year 1. A date is `YYYY-MM-DD` with a year from `0001` to `9999`; dates in the results use the same format.

Report an error (throw an exception, or return an error in Go) for the whole call if an operation is unknown or has the wrong number of arguments, a rule is malformed, a date does not follow the format or does not exist, `from` is later than `to`, the `id` of `add` already exists, or the `id` of another operation does not.

Example: `["add", "count", "skip", "next"]`, `[["party", "day 13 Fr"], ["party", "2024-01-01", "2024-12-31"], ["party", "2024-09-13"], ["party", "2024-09-01"]]` → `["ok", "2", "ok", "2024-12-13"]`.
