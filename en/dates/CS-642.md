# ISO 8601 durations one after another

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Dates and time](../dates/), [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Errors and exceptions](../errors/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-642?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You get a moment `start` as `"YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"` and a list of durations `durations` in a simplified ISO 8601 format. A duration is an optional sign `-`, the letter `P`, then, in this order, optional parts `nY` (years), `nM` (months), `nW` (weeks), `nD` (days), then an optional time part: the letter `T` followed, in order, by `nH` (hours) and `nM` (minutes). Here `n` is one to three digits; there must be at least one part, and after `T` at least one time part. For example: `"P1Y2M"`, `"P1W2D"`, `"PT36H"`, `"-P1M1DT1H"`.

Apply the durations one after another. Each is applied like this: first the years and months move the month (if the new month lacks that day, its last day is used), then weeks and days are added, then hours and minutes. With the sign `-` every part is subtracted in the same order. Return the final moment as `"YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"`; an empty list returns `start`. The calendar is Gregorian with no time zones, `start` is valid, and every intermediate year stays within 1000–9000.

If a duration does not follow these rules, report an error: throw an exception, or return an error in Go.

Example: `"2024-01-31 10:00"`, `["P1M", "P1M"]` → `"2024-03-29 10:00"`.
