# Next runs of a cron-like schedule

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Dates and time](../dates/), [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Errors and exceptions](../errors/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-630?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A schedule is a string `expression` of five fields separated by single spaces: minute (`0`–`59`), hour (`0`–`23`), day of month (`1`–`31`), month (`1`–`12`), weekday (`0`–`6`, `0` is Sunday). A field is a comma-separated list of items; an item is `*` (every value of the field), a number `a`, a range `a-b` (`a ≤ b`), or `*/k` or `a-b/k` — the values from the start taken with step `k ≥ 1`. Numbers have one or two digits and lie within the field's limits.

A moment matches when its minute, hour and month are in their fields and **both** its day of month and its weekday are in theirs (unlike classic cron, both are required). The calendar is Gregorian with no time zones. You get a moment `after` as `"YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"` (year 1900 to 2100) and a number `n ≥ 0`. Return the first `n` matching moments strictly after `after`, in increasing order and in the same format. Search only up to the end of the fourth year after the year of `after` (up to `"Y+4-12-31 23:59"` inclusive); if fewer than `n` moments match there, return those found.

If there are not five fields (an extra space counts), an item is malformed, a value is out of limits, `a > b`, a step is `0` or follows a single number, `after` is malformed or does not exist, or `n < 0`, report an error: throw an exception, or return an error in Go.

Example: `"30 9 * * 1-5"`, `"2024-03-08 10:00"`, `3` → `["2024-03-11 09:30", "2024-03-12 09:30", "2024-03-13 09:30"]`.
