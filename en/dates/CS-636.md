# When the shop opens again

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Dates and time](../dates/), [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Errors and exceptions](../errors/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-636?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Opening hours are a string `spec` of rules separated by `"; "` (a semicolon and a space). A rule is the days, then one space, then the times: the days are a comma-separated list of `Mo`, `Tu`, `We`, `Th`, `Fr`, `Sa`, `Su` or ranges like `Mo-Fr` (a range may wrap past Sunday: `Fr-Mo` is Friday, Saturday, Sunday and Monday); the times are `off` or a comma-separated list of spans `"HH:MM-HH:MM"`. A span is open from its start inclusive to its end exclusive; if the end is earlier than the start, the span is overnight and runs until the end on the next day (`22:00-02:00`). A later rule fully replaces the hours of the days it names; days never named are closed.

You get a moment `moment` as `"YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"`. If the shop is open at that moment (including the overnight tail of the previous day's span), return `moment` itself; otherwise return the nearest span start after `moment` in the same format, or the empty string `""` if the schedule has no span at all. The calendar is Gregorian with no time zones.

Report an error (throw an exception, or return an error in Go) if a rule is malformed (including an empty string or an extra space), a day is unknown, a time is not `HH:MM` or is outside `00:00`–`23:59`, a span's start equals its end, or `moment` is malformed or names a date that does not exist.

Example: `"Mo-Fr 09:00-13:00,14:00-18:00; Sa 10:00-02:00; Su off"`, `"2024-03-06 13:30"` → `"2024-03-06 14:00"`.
