# Resistance from three bands

**Difficulty:** easy · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Strings](../strings/), [Hash maps and sets](../hashing/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/EX-87?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Band colors encode digits: `black` 0, `brown` 1, `red` 2, `orange` 3, `yellow` 4, `green` 5, `blue` 6, `violet` 7, `grey` 8, `white` 9. Only the first three colors of the list are used (any extra ones are ignored). The first two are the digits of the main number, the third says how many zeros to append on the right. This gives the resistance in ohms.

Return it as the string `"<number> <unit>"`. The unit is the largest one that divides the resistance exactly: `gigaohms` (1,000,000,000), `megaohms` (1,000,000), `kiloohms` (1000), otherwise `ohms`. The number is the resistance divided by that unit. A resistance of 0 is written `"0 ohms"`.

Example: `["orange", "orange", "black"]` → `"33 ohms"`; `["orange", "orange", "orange"]` → `"33 kiloohms"`; `["orange", "orange", "red"]` → `"3300 ohms"`.
