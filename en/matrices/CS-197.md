# Rainwater on a height map

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Matrices and grids](../matrices/), [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-197?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are given a rectangular height map: `height_map[r][c]` is the height of the column in cell `(r, c)`, an integer from 0 to 20,000. The map has at most 100 × 100 cells, and all rows have the same length. After rain, water can stand above cells, but it drains off the map through any cell on its edge and flows between cells that share a side (not diagonally). Return the total volume of water that stays on the map: the sum over all cells of the depth of water above the column. An empty map and a map with fewer than three rows or columns hold no water: return `0`.

Example: `[[3, 3, 3, 3], [3, 1, 0, 3], [3, 3, 3, 3]]` → `5` (2 above the first pit and 3 above the second).
