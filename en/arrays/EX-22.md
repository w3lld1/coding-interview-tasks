# Laser and prisms

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Arrays and lists](../arrays/), [Functions and closures](../functions/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/EX-22?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A laser beam starts at point `(x, y)` and travels at `angle` degrees: 0° is right along the x axis, 90° is up, angles grow counter-clockwise. You get the beam start `start = [x, y, angle]` and a list of prisms, each prism is `[id, x, y, angle]`.

The beam travels in a straight line and hits the nearest prism ahead (a prism lies on the path when the distance from its point to the beam's line is at most `0.01`; prisms behind the beam do not count). The prism the beam has just left is ignored for the next hit. On a hit the beam continues from the prism's point and its angle grows by the prism's angle. The same prism may be hit several times. Angles may be negative or larger than 360. If no prism is ahead, the beam leaves into empty space. The input guarantees that this happens after a finite number of steps.

Return the list of prism `id`s in the order of the hits. Example: `[0, 0, 0]`, `[[1, 10, 0, 90], [2, 10, 10, 0]]` → `[1, 2]`.
