# Deck of upgraded cards

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-98?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A player has `N` cards and `coins` coins. Card `i` can have a level from `1` to `K_i = len(powers[i])`; at level `j` its power is `powers[i][j-1]` (power strictly grows with the level). The card is now at level `levels[i]`. Upgrading card `i` from level `j` to `j + 1` costs `costs[i][j-1]` coins; upgrades go one level at a time, as often as the coins allow. Levels never go down.

The player spends some coins (possibly none) on upgrades and then builds a deck of exactly `deck` different cards. Return the largest possible total power of the deck. Spending coins on cards outside the deck is pointless but allowed.

Limits: `1 ≤ deck ≤ N ≤ 12`, `1 ≤ K_i ≤ 5`, `0 ≤ coins ≤ 3·10^9`, powers and costs from `1` to `10^9`; sums do not fit in 32 bits. The original game uses `deck = 8`.

Example: `10`, `1`, `[1, 1]`, `[[1, 50, 60], [30, 40]]`, `[[6, 5], [3]]` → `50` (the first card to level 2 for 6 coins).
