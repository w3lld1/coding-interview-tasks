# Bathroom stalls: the last pick

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Math](../math/), [Hash maps and sets](../hashing/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-19?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A row has `n` empty stalls, with walls on both ends of the row. People enter one at a time and each takes the stall farthest from the occupied ones. For an empty stall, `L` and `R` are the numbers of empty stalls between it and the nearest occupied stall (or wall) on the left and on the right. A person picks the stall with the largest `min(L, R)`; on a tie, the one with the largest `max(L, R)`; on a further tie, the leftmost. Nobody leaves.

`k` people come in total (`1 ≤ k ≤ n ≤ 10^15`). Return a list of two numbers `[max(L, R), min(L, R)]` for the stall chosen by the last, `k`-th person.

Example: `4`, `2` → `[1, 0]` (the first person takes the left one of the two middle stalls, the second takes its right neighbour).
