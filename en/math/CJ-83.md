# Prime sum versus product

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Math](../math/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-83?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You have a deck of cards, each showing a prime. The deck is given as a list `cards` of pairs `[p, n]`: there are exactly `n` cards showing `p`; the primes are distinct, increasing and between 2 and 499, `n ≥ 1`, and there are at least two cards in total.

Split all the cards into two non-empty groups so that the sum of the numbers in the first group equals the product of the numbers in the second (for a single card both are the number itself). The score is that common value. Return the largest possible score, or `0` if no such split exists. The deck may be huge: the numbers on all cards add up to as much as `10^15`, so enumerating splits is impossible.

Example: `[[2, 2], [3, 1], [5, 2], [7, 1], [11, 1]]` → `25` (11 + 2 + 7 + 3 + 2 = 5 · 5).
