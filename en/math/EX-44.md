# Complex numbers

**Difficulty:** easy · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/EX-44?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Implement operations on complex numbers without built-in support. A number `a + b·i` is passed as a list of two numbers `[a, b]`. The function gets an operation name `operation` and two numbers `z1`, `z2` (for operations on one number `z2` is `[0, 0]` and unused) and returns a list of numbers.

- `"real"`, `"imaginary"`: the real part `[a]`, the imaginary part `[b]`.
- `"abs"`: the modulus `[√(a² + b²)]`.
- `"conjugate"`: the conjugate `[a, -b]`.
- `"add"`, `"sub"`: the sum and difference of `z1` and `z2`, part by part.
- `"mul"`: the product `[a·c − b·d, b·c + a·d]` for `z2 = [c, d]`.
- `"div"`: the quotient `z1 / z2`, `z2` is not zero: `[(a·c + b·d) / (c² + d²), (b·c − a·d) / (c² + d²)]`.
- `"exp"`: `e` to the power `z1`, that is `eᵃ·(cos b + i·sin b)`, the result `[eᵃ·cos b, eᵃ·sin b]`.

A real number `x` next to a complex one is written as `[x, 0]`. Numbers are compared with a tolerance of 1e-6.

Example: `"mul"`, `[1, 2]`, `[3, 4]` → `[-5, 10]`.
