# AI war: conquer and threaten

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/), [Dynamic programming](../dp/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-56?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are at war with an AI. Planets are numbered `0` to `planets − 1`; `wormholes` is a list of pairs `[x, y]` (`x < y`, no pair repeats), each a two-way wormhole between two planets. Your home planet is `0`, the AI's home planet is `1`, and `1` is always reachable from `0` through wormholes.

At first you own only planet `0`. You threaten a planet if you do not own it and a wormhole connects it to at least one planet you own. Each turn you may conquer any planet you threaten; it becomes yours. As soon as you threaten planet `1`, you stop conquering.

Reach a threat on planet `1` while conquering as few planets as possible, and among all such ways pick one that leaves you threatening the most planets at the end. Return `[c, t]`: `c` is the number of planets conquered, `t` the number of planets threatened at the end (planet `1` included). If `0` is directly connected to `1`, the answer is `[0, number of neighbours of planet 0]`. There are up to 400 planets and 2000 wormholes.

Example: `7`, `[[0,6],[0,2],[0,4],[2,4],[3,4],[2,3],[3,5],[4,5],[1,5]]` → `[2, 4]` (conquer 4 and 5, threaten 6, 2, 3 and 1).
