# Trips in rush hours

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-94?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A country has `n` cities (numbered from 1; the traveller lives in city 1) and two-way roads: `roads[i] = [x, y]`, and a pair of cities may have several roads. A road's travel time depends on the departure hour: `costs[i]` holds 24 integers from 1 to 50, and `costs[i][t]` is how many hours road `i` takes when entered at `t` o'clock (the direction does not matter). Every trip starts exactly on the hour, and the next road can be entered right on arrival; hours wrap around, so 0 follows 23. It is guaranteed that `costs[i][t] ≤ costs[i][t+1] + 1` for `t < 23` and `costs[i][23] ≤ costs[i][0] + 1`.

Each question `questions[j] = [d, s]` asks for the fewest hours to get from city 1 to city `d` when leaving at `s` o'clock (`0 ≤ s ≤ 23`). Return the answers in order: `-1` if city `d` cannot be reached, and `0` for `d = 1`. There are up to 500 cities and 2000 roads.

Example: roads `1–2` (always 1 hour), `1–3` (always 3 hours), `2–3` (always 1 hour), questions `[[2, 1], [3, 3]]` → `[1, 2]`.
