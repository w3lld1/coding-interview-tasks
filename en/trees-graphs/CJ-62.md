# Permutation from monotone run lengths

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-62?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

For a permutation `X` of the numbers `1, 2, …, n` two lists of length `n` are defined:
- `a[i]` is the length of the longest increasing subsequence that ends with `X[i]` (its largest element);
- `b[i]` is the length of the longest decreasing subsequence that starts with `X[i]` (again its largest element).

You are given `a` and `b`; at least one matching permutation is guaranteed to exist. Return the lexicographically smallest one: `X[0]` as small as possible, then `X[1]`, and so on. Limit: `1 ≤ n ≤ 2000`, so trying permutations is out.

Example: `[1, 2, 1, 3, 3, 1, 4, 1]`, `[4, 4, 3, 4, 3, 2, 2, 1]` → `[4, 5, 3, 7, 6, 2, 8, 1]`.
