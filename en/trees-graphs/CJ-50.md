# Overlaid stock charts

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/), [Matrices and grids](../matrices/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-50?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You get a list `prices`: for each of `n` stocks, its prices at the same `k` points in time. A stock's chart is the polyline through `(0, prices[i][0])`, `(1, prices[i][1])`, …, `(k − 1, prices[i][k − 1])`.

Several charts may share one overlaid chart only if no two of their polylines cross or touch. Two polylines meet that condition exactly when one stock is strictly more expensive than the other at every one of the `k` points. Return the smallest number of overlaid charts that show all the stocks (each stock on exactly one).

Limits: `1 ≤ n ≤ 100`, `1 ≤ k ≤ 25`, prices from 0 to 1 000 000.

Example: `[[1, 2, 3, 4], [2, 3, 4, 6], [6, 5, 4, 3]]` → `2`.
