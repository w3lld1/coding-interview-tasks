# Sliding tiles on a 2×3 board

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/), [Matrices and grids](../matrices/), [Hash maps and sets](../hashing/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-94?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A board of two rows of three cells is given as a list of two rows, e.g. `[[1, 2, 3], [4, 0, 5]]`; the cells hold the numbers `0` to `5`, each exactly once, and `0` is the empty cell. In one move you slide a tile that shares a side with the empty cell into it (that is, swap `0` with its neighbour above, below, left or right). The puzzle is solved when the board equals `[[1, 2, 3], [4, 5, 0]]`. Return the fewest moves to solve it; `0` if it is already solved, and `-1` if it cannot be solved.

Example: `[[4, 1, 2], [5, 0, 3]]` → `5`.
