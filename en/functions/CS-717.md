# Where two operation pipelines diverge

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Functions and closures](../functions/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-717?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A number goes through a pipeline of operations one by one. The operations are `"add K"` (add `K`), `"mul K"` (multiply by `K`), `"neg"` (change the sign), `"abs"` (absolute value), `"max K"` (take `max(x, K)`), `"min K"` (take `min(x, K)`). You are given two pipelines `left` and `right` (lists of operations; an empty list leaves the number as it is). Both are applied to the same integer `x`.

If the results are equal for all integers `x` (any, with no bound on the size), return `[]`. Otherwise find the integer `x` with the smallest `|x|` at which the results differ (between `x` and `−x`, take the negative one) and return `[x, left(x), right(x)]`.

Limits: up to 12 operations in each pipeline, `|K| ≤ 10^6`, the product of `|K|` over all `mul` operations of one pipeline is at most `10^6` (`mul 0` is allowed). The answer fits in a 64-bit integer.

Example: `["abs", "min 5"]`, `["min 5", "abs"]` → `[-6, 5, 6]`.
