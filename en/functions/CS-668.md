# SKI combinator reduction with a step limit

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Functions and closures](../functions/), [Recursion and backtracking](../recursion/), [Parsing and validation](../parsing/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-668?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A combinatory logic term `term_text` is written with characters: the capitals `S`, `K`, `I`, `B`, `C` are combinators, lowercase Latin letters are variables; application is juxtaposition and associates to the left (`abc` means `(ab)c`), parentheses group, spaces are ignored. Reduction rules (each use of a rule is one step):
- `I x → x`;
- `K x y → x`;
- `S x y z → x z (y z)`;
- `B x y z → x (y z)`;
- `C x y z → x z y`.
Here `x`, `y`, `z` are any terms. A redex is a combinator applied to at least as many arguments as its rule needs.

Reduce in normal order: always contract the leftmost outermost redex. In other words, if the term is a head applied to arguments `a1 … an` and the head is a combinator with enough arguments, it is the one contracted; otherwise the arguments are searched left to right for the first one containing a redex, and the step is made inside it by the same rule.

Make at most `limit` steps. If the term reached a normal form (no redexes), return it in canonical form: no spaces, with parentheses only around an argument that is itself an application (`xz(yz)`). If redexes remain after `limit` steps, return `"diverges"`. In the tests a term is always shorter than 2000 characters and `0 ≤ limit ≤ 1000`.

Example: `"S(Kx)(Ky)z"`, `10` → `"xy"`.
