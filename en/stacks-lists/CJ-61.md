# Swapping subway entry cards

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Stacks, queues, linked lists](../stacks-lists/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-61?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A subway line has `stations` stations numbered from 1 to `stations`; the train runs from the first to the last. On entry a passenger takes a card with the entry station; on exit they hand in some card and pay for the distance `d` between the station on the card and the exit station: the first station of the trip costs `N`, the second `N − 1`, the `i`-th `N + 1 − i` (with `N = stations`), so `d·N − d·(d − 1)/2` in total; `d = 0` is free.

You are given the list `journeys` of triples `[o, e, p]`: `p` passengers ride from station `o` to station `e` (`o < e`). Passengers may swap cards any number of times with anyone on the train at the same time, including people who leave at their entry station or enter at their exit station. Everyone leaves at their own station and hands in exactly one card. The city would collect `honest` without swaps and `paid` under the swaps that minimise the total fare. Return the loss `honest − paid` modulo `1000002013`.

Limits: `2 ≤ stations ≤ 10^9`, up to 1000 journeys, `1 ≤ p ≤ 10^9`; the sums are huge, so work modulo.

Example: `6`, `[[1, 3, 1], [3, 6, 1]]` → `6` (the two passengers swap at station 3).
