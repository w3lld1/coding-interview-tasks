# Ugly expressions from digits

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Dynamic programming](../dp/), [Math](../math/), [Strings](../strings/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-48?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Call an integer ugly if it is divisible by at least one of 2, 3, 5 or 7. Zero is ugly, and negative numbers can be ugly too (for example, −14).

You get a string `digits` of `D` digits. Between each two neighbouring digits put a plus, a minus or nothing, which gives one of `3^(D−1)` expressions. Numbers in an expression may have leading zeros: for `"01023"`, `01023`, `0+1-02+3` and `01-023` are all valid. Return how many of these expressions have an ugly value.

Limits: `1 ≤ D ≤ 30`; the values themselves can be up to 30 digits long.

Example: `"011"` → `6`.
