# Frog crossing on stones

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Dynamic programming](../dp/), [Hash maps and sets](../hashing/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-186?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Stones lie across a river; the list `stones` holds their positions in strictly increasing order, the first stone is always at `0` (the frog sits there) and the last one is on the far bank. The frog only jumps forward and only from stone to stone. Its first jump has length exactly `1`. If the previous jump had length `k`, the next one may have length `k - 1`, `k` or `k + 1` (always positive). Return `true` if the frog can reach the last stone, otherwise `false`. With a single stone the frog is already there: the answer is `true`. There are up to 2000 stones with positions up to 10⁹.

Example: `[0, 1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 12, 17]` → `true` (jumps 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5).
