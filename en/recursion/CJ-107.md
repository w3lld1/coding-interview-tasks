# Painters' duel

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Recursion and backtracking](../recursion/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/), [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-107?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A museum is an equilateral triangle of side `side`, split into `side²` equal triangular rooms. A room is a pair `[r, p]`: row `r` from `1` to `side` top to bottom, position `p` from `1` to `2r - 1` left to right. Rooms with odd `p` point up, rooms with even `p` point down. Doors connect only rooms that share a side: `[r, p]` and `[r, p + 1]` are neighbours, and a room with odd `p` and `r < side` is also a neighbour of `[r + 1, p + 1]` below it.

Alma stands in room `alma`, Berthe in a different room `berthe`; both have already painted their starting rooms. The rooms in `blocked` are under construction and cannot be entered. They take turns, Alma first. On her turn a painter must move to an adjacent room that is neither painted nor blocked and paint it; if there is none, she skips the turn. The game ends when neither can move. The score is the number of rooms Alma painted minus the number Berthe painted (starting rooms included). Alma maximises the score, Berthe minimises it, both optimally. Return the final score.

Limits: `2 ≤ side ≤ 6`; the starting and blocked rooms are all distinct.

Example: `2`, `[1, 1]`, `[2, 1]`, `[]` → `2` (Alma moves to `[2, 2]`, then to `[2, 3]`, while Berthe is stuck).
