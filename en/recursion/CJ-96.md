# Elf tournament without sad matches

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Recursion and backtracking](../recursion/), [Dynamic programming](../dp/), [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CJ-96?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A knockout tournament has `2^n` elves numbered `1` to `2^n`. Before it starts you line them up in any order. In every round the first remaining elf plays the second, the third plays the fourth, and so on; losers leave, winners keep their places. After `n` rounds one elf is left.

Some elves are sensitive. A rule `[e, k, f1, f2, …]` means elf `e` gets sad if, in one of the first `k` rounds, it plays one of its friends `f1, f2, …`. Friendship need not be mutual, but such a match makes `e` sad either way. Match results are not known in advance.

Return `true` if some line-up guarantees that no elf gets sad whatever the results are, otherwise `false`. With no rules the answer is `true`.

Limits: `1 ≤ n ≤ 4`, `1 ≤ k ≤ n`; each elf has at most one rule, with at least one friend; friends are distinct and differ from `e`; all rules together list at most `2^n` friends.

Example: `2`, `[[1, 1, 2], [3, 1, 4]]` → `true` (line-up `1, 3, 2, 4`).
