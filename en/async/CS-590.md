# Which worker took the job

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-590?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are given a list of jobs `jobs` and a number of workers `workers` (at least 1). A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). The workers are numbered from `0`; each one does one job at a time, from its start to its end (with a value or an error).

Jobs are taken strictly in index order. A job gets the free worker with the lowest number; if all are busy, it waits and starts at the moment the first worker is freed. Every job runs in the end; a failure only frees the worker. Every job takes at least 1 ms, and in the tests no two workers are freed at the same moment while jobs are waiting (except moment 0, when all are free).

Return a list of `len(jobs)` numbers: the number of the worker for each job. For an empty list return `[]`. The current time in milliseconds: `Date.now()` in JS/TS, `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. Up to 50 jobs.

Example: jobs that take 10, 20 and 30 ms, `workers = 2` → `[0, 1, 0]` (the third job takes worker `0`, freed at 10 ms).
