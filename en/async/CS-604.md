# Graceful pool stop

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-604?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are given a list of jobs `jobs`, a number `limit ≥ 1` and a stop moment `stop ≥ 0` (ms after the call of your function). A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw).

Run the jobs in a pool: at most `limit` jobs work at once, they start strictly in index order, and as soon as any job ends (with a value or an error) the next index takes its place at that same moment. But from the moment `stop` inclusive no new job starts: the ones already running are carried to the end, the rest are never called.

Return a list of three numbers: how many jobs were started, how many of them succeeded, and the moment (ms from the call of the function) at which the last started job ended (`0` if nothing was started). The current time in milliseconds: `Date.now()` in JS/TS, `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. For an empty list return `[0, 0, 0]`. Up to 50 jobs.

Example: jobs that take 10, 20 and 30 ms and return `1`, `2`, `3`, `limit = 2`, `stop = 15` → `[3, 3, 40]` (the third starts at 10 ms, before the stop, and ends at 40 ms).
