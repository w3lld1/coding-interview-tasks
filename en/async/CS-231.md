# Coroutines with several locks and deadlock

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Trees and graphs](../trees-graphs/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-231?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

There are locks numbered with integers from 0 to 9. Coroutine `i` starts at moment `starts[i]` and takes the locks `plans[i][0], plans[i][1], …` one by one, keeping the ones it already has: after getting lock `plans[i][k]` it works for `holds[i][k]` ms and only then asks for the next one. When the time after its last lock is over, the coroutine finishes and at that moment releases all its locks. A coroutine with an empty plan finishes at its start.

Each lock has its own queue: a busy lock goes to the waiting coroutines strictly in the order they asked (those asking at the same moment in increasing index order). Within one moment, first the coroutines whose time is over finish (in increasing index order), and every lock released passes at once to the first one in its queue; then the other coroutines of this moment (those starting and those that finished a step) ask for their locks in increasing index order.

Coroutines may deadlock: for example, one holds lock 0 and waits for 1 while another holds 1 and waits for 0. Such coroutines, and everyone waiting behind them (directly or through a chain), never finish. Return, for each coroutine, the moment it finished, or `-1` if it never finishes.

The function is async: it is convenient to handle the events in time order and wait for each with a timer; the current time in milliseconds is `Date.now()` in JS/TS and `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. In the tests, waits run on a virtual clock and take no real time. Limits: up to 30 coroutines, `starts[i]` from 0 to 100000, a plan has up to 5 different lock numbers with no repeats, `holds[i]` has the length of `plans[i]` and numbers from 1 to 1000000. For empty lists the answer is `[]`.

Example: `starts = [0, 0, 1]`, `plans = [[0, 1], [1, 0], [2]]`, `holds = [[2, 2], [2, 2], [3]]` → `[-1, -1, 4]` (at moment 2 coroutine 0 holds 0 and waits for 1, while coroutine 1 holds 1 and waits for 0).
