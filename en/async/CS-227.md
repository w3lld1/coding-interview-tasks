# Bounded buffer with a producer and consumers

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Stacks, queues, linked lists](../stacks-lists/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-227?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A producer makes items `0, 1, …, n−1` one by one and puts them into a shared buffer that holds at most `capacity` items. Making item `i` takes `produce[i]` ms; item 0 is started at moment 0 and each next item right after the previous one was put into the buffer. If the buffer is full, the producer waits with the finished item until a slot frees up.

`consumers` consumers take items from the buffer strictly in the order they were made: a free consumer at once takes the oldest item in the buffer, processes item `i` for exactly `consume[i]` ms and is then free again. Taking an item frees its slot at the same moment: if the producer is waiting or has just finished an item at that moment, the item goes into the buffer at that moment, and a free consumer takes an item that was just put in at that moment too.

Return, for each item in index order, the moment its processing finished, in ms from the start. The function is async: it is convenient to run the producer and the consumers as separate coroutines and wait with timers; the current time in milliseconds is `Date.now()` in JS/TS and `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. In the tests, waits run on a virtual clock and take no real time. The lists `produce` and `consume` have the same length, up to 40 items, times are integers from 0 to 100000, `capacity` and `consumers` are from 1 to 10. For empty lists return an empty list.

Example: `produce = [1, 1, 1]`, `capacity = 1`, `consume = [5, 5, 5]`, `consumers = 1` → `[6, 11, 16]` (item 2 is ready at moment 3, but a slot in the buffer frees up only at 6).
