# Running jobs in batches

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-605?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are given a list of jobs `jobs`, arrival moments `arrivals` (ms after the call of your function, non-decreasing), a batch size `size ≥ 1` and a maximum wait `wait ≥ 1`. A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). Job `i` arrives at moment `arrivals[i]`, but it does not start at once: it starts together with its batch.

A batch opens with the arrival of the first job that did not fit in the previous batch, and is closed ("flushed") at the earlier of two moments: when it holds `size` jobs (the arrival moment of the last of them) or when `wait` ms have passed since the arrival of its first job. A job that arrives no later than the wait flush moment joins the batch while it holds fewer than `size` jobs. At the flush moment all jobs of the batch start together, and the next batch does not wait for the previous one to end. In the tests no job arrives exactly at the moment `the first job of a batch + wait`.

Return the values of the jobs that succeeded, in the order they finished; equal moments by increasing index. For an empty list return `[]`. The current time in milliseconds: `Date.now()` in JS/TS, `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. Up to 50 jobs.

Example: jobs of 5 ms return `1`, `2`, `3`, `4`, arrive at 0, 3, 50 and 52 ms, `size = 3`, `wait = 20` → `[1, 2, 3, 4]` (the batch `[1, 2]` is flushed at 20 ms, `[3, 4]` at 70 ms).
