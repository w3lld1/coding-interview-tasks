# Releasing results in index order

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-596?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are given a list of jobs `jobs` and a number `limit ≥ 1`. A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw).

Run the jobs in a pool: at most `limit` jobs work at once, they start strictly in index order, and as soon as any job ends (with a value or an error), the next index takes its place.

Results are released to a consumer in index order: the result of job `i` may be released only when all jobs `0..i` have ended. The release moment of a successful job `i` is the largest of the end moments of jobs `0..i`. A failed job releases nothing, but its end, like any other, holds back the ones after it.

Return a list of `len(jobs)` numbers: the release moment (ms from the call of the function) for each job, and `-1` for a failed job. For an empty list return `[]`. The current time in milliseconds: `Date.now()` in JS/TS, `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. Up to 50 jobs.

Example: jobs that take 30, 10 and 20 ms, `limit = 2` → `[30, 30, 30]` (the second ends at 10 ms but waits for the first; the third starts at 10 ms and ends at 30 ms).
