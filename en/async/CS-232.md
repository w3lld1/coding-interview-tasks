# Fair tenant job pool with retries

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Stacks, queues, linked lists](../stacks-lists/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-232?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Given a list of strings `tenants`, a list of jobs `jobs` of the same length and numbers `limit`, `per_tenant` and `attempts` (all at least 1). A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). Job `i` belongs to tenant `tenants[i]`. Each call of a job is a try; at most `limit` tries run at the same time in total, and at most `per_tenant` tries of one tenant.

Each tenant has its own queue, which first holds its jobs by increasing index. The tenants stand in a circle in order of first appearance in `tenants`, and a pointer first stands at the first one. While a slot is free, look through the tenants around the circle starting at the pointer and find the first one whose queue is not empty and who runs fewer than `per_tenant` tries; start the first job of its queue (removing it from the queue) and move the pointer to the tenant after it. If there is no such tenant, wait.

A try of job `i` succeeded: record the event `"<i>:ok"`. It failed and the job had fewer than `attempts` tries: put the job at the end of its tenant's queue; it already had `attempts` tries: record `"<i>:gave up"`. Tries that end at the same moment are handled by increasing job index, and free slots are taken only after all of them.

Return the events in the order they happened, after all tries have ended; for empty lists return `[]`. Up to 50 jobs, each try takes at least 1 ms. In the tests, waits run on a virtual clock and take no real time; the current time in milliseconds is `Date.now()` in JS/TS and `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python.

Example: `tenants = ["a", "a", "b"]`, jobs that take 10, 5 and 30 ms, the second always fails, `limit = 2`, `per_tenant = 2`, `attempts = 2` → `["0:ok", "1:gave up", "2:ok"]` (jobs 0 and 2 start at moment 0, job 1 tries at 10 and at 15 ms).
