# Scheduler with deadlines, retries and backoff

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-233?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Given a list of jobs `jobs`, a list `deadlines` of the same length and numbers `limit`, `attempts` and `delay` (all at least 1). A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). Each call of a job is a try; at most `limit` tries run at the same time. The deadline of job `i` is moment `deadlines[i]` (in ms from the call of the function).

At first all jobs are waiting. While a slot is free, start the waiting job with the earliest deadline (on a tie, the smaller index); a try may start only strictly before the deadline. A successful try gives the job its answer, the try's value. A try still running at the deadline is cancelled at that moment (in JS/TS its result is simply ignored), its slot is freed, and the job's answer is `-1`. If the `k`-th failed try of a job (`k` from 0) failed at moment `t` and the job had fewer than `attempts` tries, the job becomes waiting again at moment `t + delay·2^k`; during the backoff it holds no slot. The answer is also `-1` for a job that has `attempts` tries after a failure, whose backoff ends no earlier than its deadline, or that is still waiting at its deadline.

All events of one moment (tries ending and cancelled, backoffs ending, deadlines) count together, and only then are free slots taken. Return the answers in job order; for empty lists return `[]`. Up to 50 jobs, each try takes at least 1 ms, deadlines from 1 to 10^6; in the tests no try ends exactly at its job's deadline. In the tests, waits run on a virtual clock and take no real time; the current time in milliseconds is `Date.now()` in JS/TS and `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python.

Example: job 0 takes 10 ms, its first call fails, later calls return `4`; job 1 takes 20 ms and returns `8`; `deadlines = [100, 200]`, `limit = 1`, `attempts = 2`, `delay = 5` → `[4, 8]` (job 0 fails at 10, job 1 runs from 10 to 30, job 0 waits from 15 and runs from 30 to 40).
