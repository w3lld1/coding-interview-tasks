# Leading and trailing throttle

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Functions and closures](../functions/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-214?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

A function is called at the moments `events` (milliseconds from the call of your function, non-decreasing; equal moments may occur). Its throttle wrapper runs it at most once per `interval` ms and does not lose the last call:

- if the function has never run or at least `interval` ms have passed since its last run, the call runs it at once, at that moment;
- otherwise the call is postponed: the function runs once at the moment "last run + `interval`", however many calls were postponed in that time;
- if a new call arrives exactly at the moment of the postponed run, they merge into one run at that moment. Calls that arrive at the same moment are handled one by one: the first merges with the postponed run, the following ones already fall into the new interval and are postponed (so `[0, 0, 0]` with `interval = 5` gives `[0, 5]`).

A postponed run is a run too: the next interval counts from it. Your function is async: model the wrapper with timers (the current time in milliseconds is `Date.now()` in JS/TS and `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python) or compute the answer otherwise, and return the moments of all runs in increasing order. For an empty `events` the answer is `[]`. Limits: `interval ≥ 1`, up to 100 calls, moments up to 10⁶.

Example: `[0, 3, 14, 15, 40]`, `interval = 10` → `[0, 10, 20, 40]`.
