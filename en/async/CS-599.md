# Prefetching items to process

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-599?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are given a list of jobs `jobs` (loads of items), a processing time `work ≥ 1` and a window size `ahead ≥ 1`. A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). One consumer processes the items in index order. For item `i` it waits for the load to end, then spends `work` ms processing it, and moves on to the next one.

Loads run ahead and in parallel: the load of item `j` starts at moment `0` if `j < ahead`, otherwise at the moment the consumer took item `j − ahead` into work. The consumer takes an item into work at the moment `max(when it became free, when the item was loaded)`. An item whose load failed is not processed: the consumer takes it at that same moment by the same rule and is free at once, and it adds nothing to the sum.

Return a list of two numbers: the sum of the values of the processed items and the moment (ms from the call of the function) when the consumer became free for the last time. For an empty list return `[0, 0]`. Up to 50 jobs.

Example: loads of 10 ms return `1`, `2`, `3`, `work = 5`, `ahead = 2` → `[6, 25]` (the consumer takes the items at 10, 15 and 20 ms: the third load starts at 10 ms and ends at 20 ms).
