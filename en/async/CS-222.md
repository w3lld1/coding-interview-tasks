# Job pool with priorities and arrivals

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Searching and sorting](../search-sort/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-222?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Given lists of equal length `arrivals`, `jobs` and `priorities` and a number `limit` (at least 1). A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). Job i arrives at moment `arrivals[i]` (in milliseconds from the call of the function; the list is not necessarily sorted) and is ready to start from then on. At most `limit` jobs run at the same time; a job runs from its start until it finishes, successfully or with an error, and frees its slot at the moment it finishes.

Whenever there is a free slot and a ready job that has not started, immediately start the ready job with the highest priority `priorities[i]`; on a tie, the one that arrived earlier, and on equal arrival moments, the one with the smaller index. Jobs arriving at the same moment become ready together, before the choice. Return the indexes of the jobs in start order (jobs started at the same moment in the order they were chosen), and wait until all jobs finish. For empty lists return `[]`.

The lists hold up to 50 items, priorities are integers, possibly negative. In the tests every job runs at least 1 ms, and no job finishes at a moment when some job arrives.

Example: `arrivals = [0, 1, 2, 3]`, jobs that take 10, 5, 5 and 5 ms, `priorities = [1, 1, 5, 3]`, `limit = 1` → `[0, 2, 3, 1]`.
