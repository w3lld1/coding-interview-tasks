# Chess clock

**Difficulty:** easy · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-701?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Two people play with a chess clock: each player has `budget` ms for the whole game. The moves `moves` are made strictly in turn: move `0` is player `0`'s, move `1` is player `1`'s, move `2` is player `0`'s again, and so on. A move is a job; it lasts while the job runs, and a failure also ends the move. A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw).

During a move only the moving player's clock runs. If their time runs out before the move ended (or exactly when it ended), they lose at the moment the time ran out: do not wait for the move to end, and make no further moves. Return the list `[number of the loser, moment]`: the moment the time ran out, or, if every move was made in time, `[-1, end moment of the last move]`; moments in milliseconds from the call of the function. For an empty list return `[-1, 0]`. The current time in milliseconds: `Date.now()` in JS/TS, `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python; in the tests the clock is virtual and waits take no real time. Up to 50 moves, `budget ≥ 1`.

Example: moves of 30, 40, 70 ms, `budget = 100` → `[0, 140]` (player `0` had 70 ms left, and their second move used them up).
