# Job pool with weights

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-589?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You are given a list of jobs `jobs`, a list `weights` of their "weights" (the same length, `1 ≤ weights[i] ≤ capacity`) and a number `capacity`. A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). While a job runs (from its start to its end, with a value or an error) it takes `weights[i]` units of a resource; the total weight of the jobs running at once must not exceed `capacity`.

Start the jobs strictly in index order: job `i + 1` does not start before job `i`, even if there is room for it. A job starts at the first moment when enough of the resource is free, including the very moment when room was freed.

Return the values of the jobs that succeeded, in the order they finished; jobs that finished at the same moment go in increasing index order. For an empty list return `[]`. The current time in milliseconds: `Date.now()` in JS/TS, `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. Up to 50 jobs.

Example: jobs that take 10, 10 and 5 ms and return `1`, `2`, `3`, weights `[2, 2, 1]`, `capacity = 3` → `[1, 3, 2]` (the second job waits until 10 ms, the third starts together with it and ends at 15 ms).
