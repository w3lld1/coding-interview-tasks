# Starts under two limiters

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** Python, JavaScript, TypeScript · **Topics:** [Async and concurrency](../async/), [Arrays and lists](../arrays/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-221?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Given a list of jobs `jobs` and three numbers, each at least 1: `limit`, `per_window` and `window` (in milliseconds). A job is an async function with no arguments: call it (`await job()`) to run it; it may fail (throw). Start the jobs strictly in index order (job i cannot start before job i − 1) and obey two limits at once.

1. At most `limit` jobs run at the same time. A job runs from its start until it finishes, successfully or with an error; its slot frees at the moment it finishes, and the next job may take it at that same moment.
2. At most `per_window` jobs start within any `window` ms: a job may start at moment t only if fewer than `per_window` jobs started before it in the interval `(t − window, t]`. In other words, job i starts no earlier than `window` ms after job `i − per_window` started.

Start every job at the earliest moment both limits allow, and wait until all of them finish. Return the start moments in milliseconds from the call of the function, in index order; for an empty list, `[]`. The current time in milliseconds: `Date.now()` in JS/TS, `round(asyncio.get_running_loop().time() * 1000)` in Python. The list holds up to 50 jobs.

Example: jobs that take 10, 10, 300, 10 and 10 ms, `limit = 2`, `per_window = 3`, `window = 100` → `[0, 0, 10, 100, 110]` (job 3 waits for the window until 100 ms, job 4 for a free slot until 110 ms).
