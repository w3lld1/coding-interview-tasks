# Count the atoms of a chemical formula

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Stacks, queues, linked lists](../stacks-lists/), [Hash maps and sets](../hashing/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-195?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Given a valid chemical formula. An element name is an uppercase Latin letter, possibly followed by several lowercase ones (`H`, `Fe`, `Uuo`). An element, and also a closing bracket, may be followed by a positive integer without leading zeros, its count (with no number the count is 1). A group in round `()` or square `[]` brackets is multiplied by the number after the bracket; brackets nest and are closed by a bracket of the same kind. Besides, the formula may consist of several parts separated by `*` (for example, the hydrate `CuSO4*5H2O`); `*` appears only outside brackets, and a part may start with a positive coefficient that multiplies the whole part.

Count how many atoms of each element the whole formula has and return a string: the elements in increasing order of their names (by character codes, so `C` < `Ca` < `Cl`), each followed by its total count if that is greater than 1.

The formula is non-empty, up to 1000 characters; every total count is at most 10⁹.

Example: `"K4[Fe(CN)6]"` → `"C6FeK4N6"`; `"CuSO4*5H2O"` → `"CuH10O9S"`.
