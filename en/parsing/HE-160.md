# Evaluate an expression from two lists

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/HE-160?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You get a list of operators and a list of non-negative integer operands; there is one operator fewer than operands, and at least one operator. Operators are `"+"`, `"-"`, `"*"`, `"//"` (integer division) and `"**"` (exponentiation). Build the expression by placing the operators between the operands in order, and return its value with the usual precedence: `**` first (a chain of powers is evaluated right to left), then `*` and `//` left to right, then `+` and `-` left to right. The divisor of `//` is always greater than zero.

Example: `["+", "*", "-"]`, `[2, 3, 4, 5]` → `9` (the expression `2 + 3 * 4 - 5`).
