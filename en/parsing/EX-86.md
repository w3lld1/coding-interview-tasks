# Resistor label with tolerance

**Difficulty:** medium · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Strings](../strings/), [Hash maps and sets](../hashing/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/EX-86?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Band colors encode digits: `black` 0, `brown` 1, `red` 2, `orange` 3, `yellow` 4, `green` 5, `blue` 6, `violet` 7, `grey` 8, `white` 9. You get a list of 1, 4 or 5 colors.

- One color (always `black`): the result is `"0 ohms"`, with no tolerance.
- Four colors: the first two are the digits of the main value, the third is the multiplier (the number of zeros appended), the fourth is the tolerance.
- Five colors: the first three are the digits of the main value, the fourth is the multiplier, the fifth is the tolerance.

Tolerance by the color of the last band: `grey` ±0.05%, `violet` ±0.1%, `blue` ±0.25%, `green` ±0.5%, `brown` ±1%, `red` ±2%, `gold` ±5%, `silver` ±10%.

Write the resistance with a prefix: below 1000 — `ohms`; from 1000 — `kiloohms` (divide by 1000); from 1,000,000 — `megaohms`; from 1,000,000,000 — `gigaohms`. The number has no extra zeros: a whole number without a point, otherwise a decimal fraction without trailing zeros. The answer format is `"<value> <unit> ±<tolerance>%"`.

Example: `["orange", "orange", "black", "green"]` → `"33 ohms ±0.5%"`; `["brown", "black", "brown", "yellow", "violet"]` → `"1.01 megaohms ±0.1%"`.
