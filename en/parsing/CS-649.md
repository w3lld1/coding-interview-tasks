# Calculator with precedence and clear errors

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Errors and exceptions](../errors/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-649?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Evaluate an integer expression `expr` (up to 200 characters). It contains non-negative integers (runs of digits, leading zeros allowed), the operators `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, `%`, `^`, parentheses and spaces; spaces between tokens are ignored, but two numbers in a row (`1 2`) are an error.

Precedence, from weakest to strongest:
1. `+` and `-`, left to right;
2. `*`, `/`, `%`, left to right;
3. unary minus (may repeat: `--5` = `5`);
4. `^`, right to left; its right operand may start with a unary minus: `2 ^ 3 ^ 2` = `2 ^ 9`, and `-2 ^ 2` = `-(2 ^ 2)` = `-4`.

`/` is division truncated toward zero (`7 / -2` = `-3`), `%` is the remainder with the sign of the dividend (`-7 % 3` = `-1`), `0 ^ 0` = `1`. There is no unary plus. For a valid expression every intermediate result is guaranteed to be below `2^53` in absolute value.

Report an error: throw an exception, or return an error in Go, if the expression is empty, contains any other character, breaks the grammar (a missing operand, an extra or unclosed parenthesis, empty parentheses, extra tokens at the end), on division or remainder by zero, and on a negative exponent.

Example: `"2 * -3 ^ 2"` → `-18`.
