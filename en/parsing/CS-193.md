# Number from English words

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Strings](../strings/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-193?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

The canonical English spelling of an integer from 0 to 999 999 999 999 999 is built as follows. The number 0 is `zero`. Otherwise the number is split into groups of three digits from the right; each non-zero group is written in words followed by its scale word: `trillion`, `billion`, `million`, `thousand`, while the last group has no scale word. Groups go from the highest to the lowest, and zero groups are skipped. A group from 1 to 999: if its hundreds digit h is non-zero, it starts with the word of the digit h and `hundred`; then, if the remainder r is non-zero, comes the spelling of r: for 1–19 one word `one` … `nineteen`, for 20–99 a tens word `twenty` … `ninety`, followed by a hyphen and the digit when the units are non-zero (`forty-two`). Words are separated by exactly one space, with no leading or trailing spaces, all in lowercase, with no `and`.

Given a string of up to 200 characters. If it is the canonical spelling of some number, return that number; otherwise return `-1`.

Example: `"two million forty-five thousand one hundred seven"` → `2045107`; `"one hundred and five"` → `-1`.
