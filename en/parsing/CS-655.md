# Date range query language

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Errors and exceptions](../errors/), [Dates and time](../dates/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-655?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

You get a query `query` that describes a set of dates in the Gregorian calendar (no time zones). Return the set of days it describes as a list of disjoint ranges in ascending order.

A query is terms separated by commas; spaces around a term are ignored. A term is a point or a range `A..B` of two points. A point is one of three kinds:
- `YYYY`, a whole year;
- `YYYY-MM`, a whole month;
- `YYYY-MM-DD`, one day.

The year is exactly 4 digits, from `1000` to `9999`, the month and the day exactly 2 digits each, and the date must exist. A range `A..B` is the days from the first day of `A` to the last day of `B` inclusive. A term with `-` right before it excludes. The answer is the union of all ordinary terms minus the union of all excluding ones (the order of terms does not matter).

Each range of the answer is maximal (adjacent and overlapping days are glued) and written as `YYYY-MM-DD..YYYY-MM-DD`; a one-day range is written just as `YYYY-MM-DD`. If no days remain, return an empty list.

Report an error: throw an exception, or return an error in Go, if the query or a term is empty, a point does not follow the format, a date does not exist, or a range's first day is after its last day.

Example: `"2024-03, -2024-03-02..2024-03-30"` → `["2024-03-01", "2024-03-31"]`.
