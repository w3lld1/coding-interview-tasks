# Calculator with units of measurement

**Difficulty:** hard · **Languages:** any of Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Go · **Topics:** [Parsing and validation](../parsing/), [Errors and exceptions](../errors/), [Math](../math/)

**[▶ Solve online on CodeShare](https://code-share.online/practice/CS-656?utm_source=github&utm_medium=tasks-repo)**

Evaluate the expression `expr` with quantities and return its value in the units `target` (a floating-point number).

Units: `mm` (0.001 m), `cm` (0.01 m), `m`, `km` (1000 m) for length; `s`, `min` (60 s), `h` (3600 s) for time; `g` (0.001 kg), `kg` for mass. Case matters; a unit is a word of consecutive Latin letters, and there are no other words.

The expression: numbers (digits, possibly with a dot and digits after it: `12`, `0.5`; `.5` and `3.` are errors); a number may be followed by a unit (spaces between them are optional: `3 m`, `1.5km`); a number without a unit is dimensionless. The operators `+`, `-`, `*`, `/` with the usual precedence and left to right, unary minus, parentheses, spaces between tokens. A unit is allowed only right after a number (`(3 + 4) m` and `2 m / s` are errors).

The dimension is tracked as powers of length, time and mass: `*` adds them and `/` subtracts them; only quantities of the same dimension may be added or subtracted. `target` is written without spaces: a unit or `1`, then zero or more pairs "`*` or `/`, a unit", for example `km/h`, `kg*m/s/s`, `1/s`; `1` may only come first. The answer is the value of the expression divided by the size of `target`; the dimensions must match.

Report an error: throw an exception, or return an error in Go, if the expression or `target` is malformed, a unit is unknown, different dimensions are added, a divisor is zero, or the dimension of the result does not match `target`.

Example: `"10 km / 2 h"`, `"m/s"` → `1.3888888888888888`.
